# PRACH Jamming

## Overview

PRACH (Physical Random Access Channel) jamming targets the very first step of the 5G random access procedure. By transmitting noise or false preambles on the PRACH frequency-time resources, an attacker prevents UEs from completing initial access.

**Ease of execution: 5/5** -- one of the simplest active attacks in the 5G threat landscape.

## Key Paper

- **Title:** PRACH Jamming in 5G NR
- **Reference:** arXiv 2410.08729, October 2024
- **Result:** Over 99.9% connection denial rate demonstrated

## How It Works

1. The attacker identifies the target cell's PRACH configuration from SIB1 (broadcast in cleartext).
2. A USRP transmits high-power noise or random preambles aligned to the PRACH occasion slots.
3. The gNB cannot decode legitimate preambles, so Msg1 of the RACH procedure fails for all UEs in range.

The attack is effective because PRACH resources are predictable, periodic, and unencrypted.

## Hardware Requirements

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| SDR | USRP B210 or N310 (paper uses OAI + B210/N310) |
| Power Amplifier | **Required** -- must overpower legitimate UE transmissions |
| Software | OpenAirInterface (OAI) or custom GNU Radio flowgraph |
| Antenna | Directional preferred for targeted jamming |

## Impact

- Complete denial of new connections in the target cell
- Existing connected UEs are unaffected until they attempt re-establishment
- Affects all UEs regardless of device or subscription type

## Mitigations

- PRACH preamble hopping (limited effectiveness if attacker jams all occasions)
- Jammer detection and localization at the gNB
- Backup frequency bands or inter-RAT fallback
