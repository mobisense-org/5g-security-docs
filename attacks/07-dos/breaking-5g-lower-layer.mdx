# Breaking 5G on The Lower Layer (2026)

## Overview

This paper presents two complementary lower-layer attacks against 5G NR: SIB1 spoofing that increases battery drain, and Timing Advance (TA) manipulation that forces UEs into a reconnection loop, causing sustained denial of service.

**Ease of execution: 4/5** -- uses commodity hardware and open-source software.

## Key Paper

- **Authors:** Shanto, Karim, Bertino (Purdue University)
- **Venue:** NDSS FutureG Workshop, February 2026
- **Reference:** arXiv 2602.10250

## Attack 1: SIB1 Spoofing for Battery Drain

1. The attacker broadcasts a spoofed SIB1 with modified cell parameters (e.g., inflated timers, altered DRX configuration).
2. UEs that camp on the rogue cell adopt the spoofed parameters.
3. The manipulated DRX settings prevent UEs from entering deep sleep, significantly increasing battery consumption.

## Attack 2: Timing Advance Manipulation for DoS

1. The attacker manipulates Timing Advance commands, either via a rogue gNB or by injecting spoofed MAC CEs.
2. The victim UE's uplink timing drifts out of the gNB's acceptable window.
3. The gNB drops the UE's uplink, forcing RRC re-establishment.
4. The attacker repeats the manipulation on reconnection, creating a persistent reconnection loop.

## Impact

- **Battery drain:** Measurable reduction in UE battery life through forced active-mode operation
- **Reconnection loop DoS:** UE cannot maintain a stable connection, effectively denying all services
- Both attacks exploit unprotected broadcast channels and MAC-layer signaling

## Mitigations

- SIB1 integrity protection or plausibility checks at the UE
- TA validation bounds at the UE MAC layer
- Multi-cell cross-referencing to detect anomalous system information
