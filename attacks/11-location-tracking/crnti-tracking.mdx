# C-RNTI Tracking

## Overview

The Cell Radio Network Temporary Identifier (C-RNTI) is assigned to each UE during RRC connection setup and used in all subsequent scheduling on PDCCH. Because RRC signaling is **not ciphered** at the time of C-RNTI assignment, and the C-RNTI appears in cleartext in every DCI message, a passive observer can track a UE's presence and activity within a cell.

**Ease of execution: 5/5** -- purely passive, no transmission, no extra hardware beyond a receive SDR.

## Key References

C-RNTI tracking is a well-established radio-layer privacy issue rather than a single-paper finding. The mechanism is documented in 3GPP itself and in multiple academic works:

- **5GSniffer** ([spritelab/5GSniffer](https://github.com/spritelab/5GSniffer), Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya) — open-source 5G NR downlink decoder that extracts C-RNTI from PDCCH in real time. The associated paper at ACM ASIA CCS 2024 demonstrates per-UE tracking from PDCCH-only captures.
- **NR-Scope** ([PrincetonUniversity/NR-Scope](https://github.com/PrincetonUniversity/NR-Scope)) — sister tool focused on 5G NR PDCCH/PDSCH analysis.

> *Earlier attribution note:* this page previously cited *"Demystifying Privacy in 5G SA"* (arXiv 2409.17700, Borgaonkar et al.). On re-read, that paper documents **GUTI Reallocation** and **Security-Capabilities Bidding-Down** — not C-RNTI tracking — so the citation has been moved to the tools that actually implement and demonstrate C-RNTI tracking.

## How It Works

1. The attacker deploys a passive SDR receiver tuned to the target 5G NR cell.
2. The receiver decodes PDCCH (DCI messages) which contain the C-RNTI of scheduled UEs.
3. By correlating C-RNTI values over time, the attacker can:
   - Detect when a specific UE is present in the cell
   - Track UE mobility across time (C-RNTI persistence within a cell)
   - Estimate activity patterns (scheduling frequency correlates with data usage)
4. Cross-cell tracking is possible if the attacker deploys receivers at multiple cells and correlates handover timing.

## Tools

- **5GSniffer:** [spritelab/5GSniffer](https://github.com/spritelab/5GSniffer) -- passive 5G NR downlink decoder
- **NR-Scope:** [PrincetonUniversity/NR-Scope](https://github.com/PrincetonUniversity/NR-Scope) -- 5G NR monitoring tool

Both tools can decode PDCCH and extract C-RNTI values from live 5G NR signals.

## Hardware Requirements

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| SDR | USRP B210 (receive only) |
| Extra hardware | **None** -- no PA, no transmission capability needed |
| Software | 5GSniffer or NR-Scope |
| Antenna | Standard receive antenna for target band |

## Limitations

- C-RNTI is cell-specific and changes on handover (cross-cell tracking requires correlation heuristics)
- C-RNTI may be reassigned when the UE re-enters RRC_CONNECTED from RRC_IDLE
- Mapping C-RNTI to a real identity requires additional side information (e.g., timing correlation with known events)

## Mitigations

- C-RNTI randomization and frequent rotation (limited by 3GPP spec constraints)
- Encrypting DCI (not in current 3GPP specifications)
- Reducing C-RNTI lifetime through aggressive RRC release timers

## RF Signature

The attacker is purely passive — *attack-in-progress detection from RF + AI is impossible* (table value: `Neither`).

A passive RF + AI sensor at the same vantage point as the attacker **can** answer the precondition question: *"how rotatable / linkable are the C-RNTIs this cell hands out?"* A defender can use 5GSniffer or NR-Scope to measure C-RNTI lifetime, rotation cadence, and DCI predictability per cell. That data feeds an exposure score for the operator's privacy posture — not an intrusion signal, but a meaningful audit signal.
