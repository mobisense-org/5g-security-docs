## Summary

*Passive Multi-Target GUTI Identification via Visual-RF Correlation in LTE Networks* (NDSS 2026) is a tracking attack that pairs a camera feed with a passive RF sniffer. Because GUTIs (and their 5G equivalent 5G-GUTI) are temporary identifiers that the network reassigns, classical IMSI-catcher tracking breaks across reallocations. This paper sidesteps the problem entirely: by correlating *visible* events (a person walking past a doorway, a car passing) with *RF* events (a paging response, a scheduling pattern, a periodic registration), the attacker resolves which GUTI belongs to which physical target — across multiple targets simultaneously.

## Attack Mechanism

1. **Passive RF capture:** Sniff LTE/5G control channels, extract per-RNTI scheduling, paging events, and registration timing.
2. **Visual capture:** Record video of a chosen physical area.
3. **Event correlation:** Cross-correlate RF events (paging arrival, uplink burst pattern, periodic registration) with visual events (person enters, leaves, takes a call). Repeated coincidences across many windows pin a specific GUTI to a specific physical target.
4. **Multi-target tracking:** Algorithm scales to multiple simultaneous targets in the same camera frame.

## Why It's Attractive

- **Pure passive:** No active radio. Undetectable by standard FBS detectors and operator-side anomaly systems.
- **Defeats GUTI rotation:** Every time the network reassigns a temporary identifier, the visual track stays continuous and re-identifies the new GUTI.
- **Cross-modal fusion is the new idea:** RF-only tracking and camera-only tracking are both well-studied; combining them removes the weaknesses of each.

## How It Relates to Other Attacks

- Complements [C-RNTI tracking](/attacks/11-location-tracking/crnti-tracking) — that work tracks within a cell using radio fingerprints; this work pins a radio identity to a *real-world* identity.
- More stealthy than [IMSI catchers](/attacks/01-imsi-catching/overview) — no transmission, no protocol manipulation.

## Defenses (limited)

GUTI rotation alone does **not** defeat this attack. Effective mitigations would have to either (a) eliminate timing side-channels in paging/scheduling or (b) randomize control-channel timing — both expensive. The paper itself is largely an existence proof rather than a defense paper.

## RF Signature

The attacker is fully passive on the RF side (camera is out-of-band), so *attack-in-progress detection from RF + AI is impossible* (table value: `Neither`).

A passive RF + AI sensor at the same vantage point **can** measure the preconditions the fusion algorithm exploits:

- **GUTI reallocation cadence** per cell — long-lived GUTIs make the fusion easier.
- **Paging determinism** — predictable paging windows / lengths give the visual side stable anchors to correlate against.
- **Per-UE scheduling regularity** that distinguishes one subscriber's traffic profile from another's.

A defender can use this to score how exposed a given cell is, even though they cannot see an attacker doing the sniff.

## Related work

The 2024 Borgaonkar et al. paper *"Demystifying Privacy in 5G Stand Alone Networks"* ([arXiv 2409.17700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17700)) is closely related on the RF side: it documents a **GUTI Reallocation Command** passive attack where the unencrypted 5G-GUTI is observable in the Configuration Update Command on the downlink paging channel — the same identifier this work then visually re-anchors.
