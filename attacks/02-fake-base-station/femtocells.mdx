## Summary

*Small Cell, Big Risk: A Security Assessment of 4G LTE Femtocells in the Wild* (NDSS 2026) is a field study of *deployed* carrier-grade femtocells — the small home/enterprise cells that operators ship to customers as Wi-Fi-style boxes plugging into a residential ISP. Unlike most fake-base-station research, which builds attack tooling in the lab, this paper audits gear that is already in homes and offices.

The attractive part isn't a single new exploit — it's that real, shipping infrastructure has the kind of weaknesses that academic threat models usually assume away.

## What They Found (high level)

The paper documents weaknesses in deployed femtocells across several axes:

- **Backhaul authentication gaps:** Some femtocells authenticate to the operator core with credentials that are extractable from the device (firmware images, secure-element bypass, debug interfaces).
- **Broadcast manipulation:** Cell selection parameters (priorities, neighbor lists) can be tampered with to influence which UEs camp on the femtocell vs. macro coverage.
- **Privileged position relative to UEs:** A compromised femtocell sits inside the operator's trust boundary — it can observe, redirect, or selectively block subscriber traffic for any UE camping on it.

## Why It's Attractive

- **Real deployments, not lab gear:** The attack surface here is something carriers actually ship today. Most academic FBS papers stand up an attacker gNB; this paper turns *legitimate* gNBs into attacker-controlled ones.
- **Bridges air-interface and supply-chain threats:** The femtocell is both a radio and a backhaul-attached IP device. Compromise crosses both worlds.
- **Relevant to private 5G:** Enterprise / private-network deployments rely on the same small-cell architecture. Findings transfer directly.

## How It Relates to Other Attacks Here

- A compromised femtocell is the strongest possible position for [SIB1 spoofing](/attacks/02-fake-base-station/sib1-spoofing), [cell reselection](/attacks/02-fake-base-station/cell-reselection), and [downgrade](/attacks/03-downgrade/bidding-down) — because it is an *authentic* cell from the UE's perspective.
