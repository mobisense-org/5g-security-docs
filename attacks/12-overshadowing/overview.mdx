## What Overshadowing Is

Overshadowing sits between passive sniffing and a full rogue gNB. The attacker does **not** stand up a fake cell; instead it transmits a signal that is precisely time- and frequency-aligned with the legitimate downlink (or uplink) and only a few dB stronger, so the victim's receiver locks onto the attacker's symbols for a *specific* message while everything else still comes from the real network. Because LTE/5G broadcast and pre-authentication control messages are not integrity-protected, the overshadowed message is accepted as genuine.

This makes overshadowing stealthier and lower-power than a rogue cell, but it requires tight synchronization to the target cell.

## Why It Matters for Passive-RF Detection

Overshadowing leaves a distinctive radio fingerprint: two time-aligned copies of the same message with a small power offset, brief constellation/EVM anomalies, and inconsistent CRC/decoding across receivers. Unlike a rogue gNB (a whole new cell appears) or passive sniffing (nothing transmits), the overshadowing transmitter *is* on the air during the attack — which is exactly what a passive RF + AI sensor is positioned to catch.
