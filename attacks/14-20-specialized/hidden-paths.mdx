## Summary

*Uncovering Hidden Paths in 5G: Exploiting Protocol Tunneling and Network Boundary Bridging* (CCS 2025) shows that the layered tunneling protocols holding a 5G network together — GTP-U, N3 / N9, PFCP — are validated inconsistently across implementations. Attackers with limited access to one segment of the network can bridge into segments they should not reach by riding inside legitimate tunnels.

This generalizes the [GTP-U hijack](/attacks/14-20-specialized/gtp-tunneling) finding ("Invade the Walled Garden", May 2025) into a broader class of tunnel-misuse attacks.

## Attack Mechanism

The paper documents several "hidden paths," all variations on the same theme:

1. **Outer-vs-inner mismatch:** A packet's outer encapsulation says it belongs to context A, but the inner payload is for context B. Some receivers validate the outer header but route based on the inner — the path crosses a trust boundary undetected.
2. **PFCP misuse:** PFCP is the SMF↔UPF control protocol; weak authentication / missing source validation on PFCP lets an attacker reachable on the same network steer user traffic.
3. **N9 cross-PLMN bridging:** In roaming scenarios, weakly validated N9 tunnels create paths between PLMNs that the operators believed were isolated.

## Why It Matters

- **Bridges classes that papers usually treat separately:** GTP, PFCP, and SBA all validated independently; this paper looks at the seams between them.
- **Real consequence:** Direct user-plane access (read/inject), session steering, and exfil from inside the operator's "walled garden."
- **Affects roaming:** N9-related findings have multi-operator impact.

## How It Relates to Other Attacks

- Builds on [GTP-U hijack](/attacks/14-20-specialized/gtp-tunneling) (Invade the Walled Garden) — same threat surface, more mechanisms.
- Pairs with [CITesting](/attacks/14-20-specialized/citesting) as part of the 2025 "5G core under siege" wave.

## Defenses

- Validate inner/outer header consistency on every tunnel termination point.
- IPsec on N3/N9 (3GPP-recommended but rarely deployed end-to-end).
- PFCP authentication (3GPP TS 29.244) with strict source policing.
