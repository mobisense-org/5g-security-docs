## Summary

Network slicing is the central promise of 5G's multi-tenant value: one physical infrastructure carrying logically isolated slices (eMBB, URLLC, IoT, enterprise, …) each with its own SLA and security policy. The attacks in this category break that isolation: a rogue gNB or compromised RAN component can forge slice-selection metadata (NSSAI), trigger slice-hopping, or use side-channels to infer activity on adjacent slices. Recent paper (Xu et al., "Integrity Under Siege," Nov 2025) demonstrates QoS degradation up to 95 % bandwidth reduction and ~80 % UPF CPU saturation.

## Threat Model

| | |
|---|---|
| **Attacker** | Rogue gNB / compromised RAN component (DU/CU) with control over NSSAI in RRC registration |
| **Victim** | 5GC slice tenants and the UPF serving them |
| **Position** | Acts as a MitM-style or rogue gNB in the RAN |
| **Capability** | Forge NSSAI in `RegistrationRequest`; exploit weak slice-validation policies in AMF; null-cipher fallback (5G-EA0) makes the attack stealthier |
| **Impact** | QoS degradation, resource exhaustion, DoS, cross-slice data exposure |

## Attack Mechanism

1. The rogue gNB intercepts (or originates) a UE registration that targets slice A (e.g., eMBB).
2. It rewrites the NSSAI to request slice B (e.g., URLLC) — a slice the UE is not authorized for.
3. If the AMF validates NSSAI loosely (common in misconfigured cores), the UE is admitted to slice B.
4. The attacker can now: (a) consume slice-B resources (DoS for legit tenants), (b) inject traffic into slice B's isolated context, (c) trigger massive cross-slice contention to saturate the UPF.

The 2025 paper measures 95 % bandwidth reduction on the targeted slice and ~60 % packet loss in cross-slice contamination attacks.

## Replication

- **Setup**: Open5GS configured with at least two slices (eMBB + URLLC). Modify srsRAN gNB to emit forged NSSAI in `RegistrationRequest`.
- **No SIM modification needed** if the attack runs at the RAN side.
- **PA required** for over-the-air testing; without PA, restricted to shielded lab.
- **Measurement**: drive load on slice A's traffic and observe degradation on slice B.

## RF Signature (passive RF + AI)

Network slicing attacks are **partially visible on the air interface** — NSSAI is carried in plaintext RRC during registration:

- **NSSAI-vs-PCI correlation**: a passive sniffer can decode the requested NSSAI and compare it to expected slice allocations per cell/operator. AI angle: classifier on (PCI, NSSAI) pairs to flag anomalous combinations.
- **Registration-request rate per slice**: rogue-gNB-driven floods register many fake UEs into a slice; a sniffer sees the registration storm.
- **Subtle but real**: the harder signal is operators rarely publish their per-cell slice maps, so the AI needs to learn the baseline empirically.

This makes slicing a **moderate-fit** target for the lab's RF + AI thesis — partially observable on air, partially core-only.

## Detection (operator side)

- AMF policy: strict NSSAI validation against the subscription profile in UDM.
- SMF/UPF: per-slice quota enforcement so a single slice can't starve the others.
- O-RAN xApp monitoring slice-allocation anomalies in near-real-time.
