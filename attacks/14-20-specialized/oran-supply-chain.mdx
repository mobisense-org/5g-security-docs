## Summary

Open RAN disaggregates the gNB into RU + DU + CU + RIC + xApps, connected by open interfaces (F1 between CU and DU, E2 between RIC and CU/DU, A1 between Non-RT and Near-RT RIC, O1 management). Each new interface is a new attack surface; each multi-vendor split is a new supply-chain seam. The NTIA Open RAN Security Report (May 2023) flagged this expansion; NDSS '24's 5G-Spector paper built the first xApp-based intrusion detection for it. Most of the surface is on internal operator networks rather than over the air.

## Threat Model

| | |
|---|---|
| **Attacker** | Malicious xApp or rApp deployed to the RIC; compromised vendor component in the supply chain; insider on the operator's internal RAN network |
| **Victim** | gNB (CU/DU), RIC, ultimately UEs in the affected cells |
| **Position** | Inside the operator's RAN management plane (E2/A1/O1) or fronthaul (eCPRI between RU and DU) |
| **Capability** | Read/write E2 SM messages; manipulate RIC policy decisions; forge fronthaul I/Q if PHY-layer encryption isn't deployed |
| **Pre-requisite** | RIC deployment access, xApp publishing rights, or fronthaul physical/network access |

## Attack Mechanism

**E2 / xApp:** A malicious xApp speaks E2-AP to the gNB. Without RIC policy enforcement, the xApp can issue control actions that move UEs between cells, change scheduling priorities, mute specific UEs, or exfiltrate per-UE metrics. The xApp is a privileged tenant by design.

**A1 / Non-RT RIC:** policies flow from the SMO into the Near-RT RIC. A tampered A1 channel rewrites policies on the fly.

**O1 / management:** O-RAN's O1 interface (over NETCONF) configures the gNB. Missing TLS or weak credentials → full reconfiguration.

**Fronthaul (eCPRI/Split 7.2x):** the I/Q traffic between RU and DU is by default unencrypted; the O-RAN security WG recommends MACsec or IPsec, but adoption varies. An attacker on the fronthaul switch sees raw radio samples.

## Replication

- **xApp testing**: deploy [5G-Spector](https://github.com/5GSEC/5G-Spector) as a benign monitoring xApp on a Near-RT RIC (ONOS RIC, FlexRIC, OSC RIC). Then deploy a deliberately misbehaving xApp to see what E2 calls it can issue. Use [MobiDojo](https://github.com/5GSEC/MobiDojo) as a virtual O-RAN testbed for safe experimentation.
- **Fronthaul testing**: requires real RU hardware. Mobisense has Benetel 550/640 O-RAN RUs which support fronthaul capture — instrument a 10 GbE tap between RU and DU.
- **Extra hardware**: PTP-grade fronthaul switch ($2k–5k), SFP+ 10 GbE NIC, optical taps for non-disruptive capture.

## RF Signature

O-RAN attacks are **mostly internal** — E2/A1/O1 traffic is on the operator network, not the air. **Two partial exceptions** the lab's RF thesis can address:

- **Fronthaul (eCPRI) eavesdropping** is over fiber/Ethernet between RU and DU, not over the air — out of scope for an over-the-air sniffer, but in scope for a wired tap.
- **Downstream RF effects** of xApp manipulation: if a malicious xApp forces a victim UE to a worse cell or mutes its scheduling, a passive RF sniffer near the victim sees the resulting handover storm or PUSCH starvation. AI angle: per-UE scheduling-pattern anomaly detection.

Net: mostly **not visible** to passive RF + AI, but downstream effects can be inferred from radio behavior.

## Detection (operator / RIC side)

- RIC: cryptographic auth on xApp registration; per-xApp scoping of E2 service models.
- A1/O1: mTLS everywhere; signed config payloads.
- Fronthaul: MACsec/IPsec on eCPRI links (per O-RAN security WG).
- Supply chain: SBOM tracking on every RAN component; signed firmware images.
