## Summary

5Gone is a Feb 2026 attack from ETH Zurich + Oxford that demonstrates the first **uplink** signal overshadowing on 5G Standalone networks. Unlike all prior overshadowing work that targeted downlink broadcasts, 5Gone injects crafted uplink signals that impersonate legitimate UE transmissions toward the gNB. It scales to **64 UEs in parallel** across a full 100 MHz NR cell with end-to-end latency under 500 microseconds. The attack runs on COTS x86 hardware — no FPGA strictly required for narrower configurations.

## Threat Model

| | |
|---|---|
| **Attacker** | SDR-based overshadower with sufficient PA gain |
| **Victim** | UE (uplink message replaced) and gNB (decodes attacker payload) |
| **Position** | Within radio range; attacker must beat the legitimate UE's power at the gNB |
| **Capability** | Time/frequency alignment to the legitimate UE's scheduled PUSCH slot (sub-symbol precision) + ability to decode downlink DCI in real time |
| **Pre-auth?** | Works at any RRC state, including pre-auth |
| **No SIM** | No SIM or subscriber credentials required |

## Attack Mechanism

5Gone exploits the fact that the gNB schedules uplink transmissions on known time-frequency resources via DCI grants. The attacker:

1. Monitors the cell's downlink DCI on PDCCH to learn exactly when and where each target UE will transmit.
2. At the scheduled instant, injects overshadowing uplink symbols on the legitimate UE's PUSCH resources.
3. Because the attacker's signal arrives stronger at the gNB (either by physical proximity or PA gain), the gNB decodes the **attacker's** payload as if it came from the legitimate UE.

Outcomes: arbitrary uplink NAS/RRC message injection, targeted DoS, downgrade signaling, identity-binding manipulation — all without an integrity check because pre-auth uplink is unprotected.

## Replication

- **Reference hardware** (paper): USRP X310 with band 78 (n78) or band 8 (n8) PAs.
- **Practical alternative**: USRP B210 + Ettus N310. B210 caps you at 56 MHz instantaneous bandwidth — fine for narrower cells, not for the full 100 MHz × 64-UE demo.
- **Timing budget**: the sub-500 μs E2E target requires FPGA-assisted DCI decode + symbol generation. Without FPGA, latency typically exceeds the slot boundary on commercial gNBs.
- **Code not public** as of Feb 2026 — replication requires reimplementing the DCI decoder + symbol injector from the paper.

## RF Signature (passive RF + AI)

This is the most interesting case for AI-based RF defense — the attacker's signal looks **almost legitimate**:

- **Power-vs-position anomaly**: the gNB sees the "legitimate UE" suddenly at impossibly high RSRP for one PUSCH slot. A passive sniffer co-located near the gNB sees the same. AI angle: train on per-UE expected uplink power profiles, flag spikes.
- **Decoder-confidence dips on neighbors**: 64-UE parallel injection creates interference on adjacent PUSCH allocations. Subtle but measurable via channel-quality metrics.
- **Bit-level anomalies in NAS/RRC content**: the injected payloads often violate state-machine expectations (e.g., uplink Detach without prior Registration). AI angle: sequence classifiers over decoded uplink messages.
- **Hardest to catch passively**: when the attack overshadows a single UE silently, the only signal is the impossibility of two-source-decoding at the gNB front-end — invisible to a third-party sniffer.

## Detection (gNB / core side)

- gNB PUCCH/PUSCH SINR/BLER spikes on specific allocations.
- AMF-side: NAS messages that violate the registered UE's state machine (e.g., re-Registration with stale GUTI).
- Cross-check expected UE radio profile (SRS, CSI reports) vs received uplink power.
