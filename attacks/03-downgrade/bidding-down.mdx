## Summary

The "Downgrade Dance" walks a victim from 5G SA down to 2G one generation at a time, then intercepts on the 2G leg where mutual authentication and strong ciphering do not exist. Each step abuses an **unprotected NAS reject message** that the UE accepts *before* a security context is established: a 5G `Registration Reject` with cause **#27 (N1 mode not allowed)** disables the UE's 5G capability, then a 4G `Tracking Area Update Reject` with cause **#42 (Severe network failure)** makes it abandon 4G and camp on a 2G cell. In the WiSec '23 study, **all 7 commercial phones (5 baseband vendors) were downgraded successfully**. The attack is design-level — it exploits the fact that pre-authentication NAS traffic cannot be verified — so it is not "patchable" as a CVE; it persists until reject-after-auth is enforced or 2G is disabled.

## Threat Model

| | |
|---|---|
| **Attacker** | Multi-RAT rogue base stations (fake 5G gNB + 4G eNB + 2G BTS) broadcasting the **operator's own PLMN** at higher signal strength; optionally a jammer on the legitimate cell |
| **Victim** | Any 5G-SA-capable UE with 2G fallback enabled (the default on most phones) |
| **Position** | Within radio range of the victim; must out-power the legit cell — by gain (lab) or by jamming the real carrier (field) |
| **Capability** | Operate a cell on each RAT and send **unprotected pre-auth NAS Reject** messages with high-impact causes |
| **Pre-auth?** | Yes — reject messages are accepted before the security context exists, so the UE cannot verify them |
| **External requirement** | A 2G fallback path must exist (legit 2G coverage, or the attacker supplies the 2G cell) |

## Attack Mechanism

The downgrade is **NAS-reject driven**, not RRC-redirect driven. In 5G, the UE is required to *ignore* a redirection field in a pre-authenticated `RRC Release` in any case, so the attacker instead exploits NAS reject causes that the UE obeys before authentication.

```
Victim on legit 5G-SA
   │  attacker out-powers the legit cell (higher gain, or jams the real carrier)
   ▼
Fake 5G gNB  (same PLMN, stronger RSRP)
   │  UE → NAS Registration Request
   │  gNB → NAS Registration Reject, cause #27 "N1 mode not allowed"
   ▼  UE disables 5G-SA capability, searches for 4G
Fake 4G eNB  (same PLMN)
   │  UE → NAS Attach / TAU Request
   │  eNB → NAS TAU Reject, cause #42 "Severe network failure"
   ▼  UE ignores all 4G cells of this PLMN, searches for 2G
Fake 2G BTS  (same PLMN)
   │  UE camps — no mutual authentication, weak/no GSM cipher
   ▼  Interception of calls / SMS / data
```

### High-impact reject causes

The paper enumerates which causes actually move a UE versus merely cause a DoS:

| Leg | Message (spec) | Cause | Effect | Result across 7 UEs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5G→4G | Registration Reject (TS 24.501) | **#27 N1 mode not allowed** | Disables 5G-SA capability entirely | **Downgrade on all 7** (TC11) |
| 5G→4G | Registration Reject | #7 5GS services not allowed | — | Downgrade 2, DoS 3 (TC12) |
| 5G→4G | Registration Reject | #11 PLMN not allowed | — | Downgrade 1, DoS 1 (TC13) |
| 4G→2G | TAU Reject (TS 24.301) | **#42 Severe network failure** | UE ignores all 4G cells of the PLMN | **Downgrade on all 7** (TC14) — *new cause identified by this paper* |
| 4G→2G | TAU Reject | #7 EPS services not allowed | — | Downgrade 3 (TC15) |
| 4G→2G | TAU Reject | #8 EPS + non-EPS not allowed | — | DoS 5 (TC16) |

Cause **#42** is the paper's notable contribution — it triggers the 4G→2G drop more reliably than the reject causes used in prior work, and was previously untested. The `RRC Release` redirection (`redirectedCarrierInfo`) is a **complementary** vector usable on the 4G→2G leg when the operator omits the GERAN-redirect policy bit, but it is *not* what carries the 5G→4G step.

## Replication

**What the paper used:** a modified **Open5GS** core + **srsENB/srsRAN** gNB/eNB, **USRP B210 / X300** SDRs, **srsUE** and **CoreScope** (srsRAN's RF-free core tester), and **SCAT + srsUE** on a rooted phone for the public-network tests. This is almost exactly your bench.

**In your lab — the 5G→4G leg is the easy, high-value reproduction (~4–5/5):**

1. Stand up the legit cell: **srsRAN gNB + Open5GS**, your provisioned SIM phones camped on 5G SA.
2. Bring up a **second srsRAN gNB as the fake 5G cell** on the same PLMN at higher gain (a B210 is plenty; raise gain to win reselection in a cabled/attenuated setup).
3. On the fake side, make the AMF answer the incoming `Registration Request` with **`Registration Reject`, 5GMM cause #27**. The cleanest path is to patch Open5GS's AMF reject handler (or a minimal fake-gNB NAS responder) to emit cause #27.
4. Observe the UE **disable 5G-SA and reselect 4G** within seconds. That is the core, fully reproducible result.

**Full 5G→2G dance (~3/5):** add a **4G leg** (srsRAN srseNB + Open5GS EPC) that replies to the `TAU/Attach Request` with **EMM cause #42**, and a **2G leg** using **Osmocom** (OsmoBTS + OsmoBSC + OsmoMSC) or **YateBTS** on a spare B210. Drive the cascade by gain manipulation (lab) or by jamming the legit carrier (field). The 2G stack is the only piece not already in your toolchain.

<Warning>
The 2G BTS and any jamming are **heavily regulated**. Run the whole chain **conducted (cabled + attenuators) or inside a shielded enclosure** on test SIMs only — the same constraint the authors describe for their shielded environment.
</Warning>

<Tip>
For a passive-RF **detection** thesis you do not need the 2G leg working end-to-end. The detectable evidence — the same-PLMN rogue cell, the pre-auth reject causes, and the RAT cascade — is all generated by the 5G→4G step, which you can reproduce entirely with srsRAN + Open5GS + your phones.
</Tip>

## RF Signature (passive RF + AI)

`Detect = Both`, and the on-air evidence is unusually rich — this is one of the stronger downgrade signatures for a passive sniffer:

- **Twin cell on the operator's PLMN at higher RSRP** on an unexpected PCI / ARFCN — the classic fake-BS tell, flagged against a baseline of legitimate PCIs.
- **Unprotected pre-authentication NAS Reject carrying a high-impact cause** — `Registration Reject #27/#7/#11` or `TAU Reject #42/#7/#8` — sent immediately after a `Registration`/`Attach` request, with no security header and no integrity. Legitimate networks essentially never send these causes pre-auth; this single field is a near-deterministic indicator.
- **A RAT-downgrade cascade for one subscriber**: 5G registration → reject → 4G attach → reject → 2G camp, all within seconds. The descending-RAT re-registration sequence is a strong temporal pattern.
- **Optional corroboration**: wideband interference on the legit 5G ARFCN (jamming) coincident with the rogue cell, and the anomalous appearance of a 2G/GSM cell in a 5G-only area.

**AI angle:** a sequence classifier over `(cell-appearance events + SIB fingerprint + NAS reject cause + RAT transitions)` cleanly separates a downgrade dance from benign mobility or a legitimate inter-RAT handover. The pre-auth **reject-cause field is the highest-signal single feature** and is readable by any NAS-aware downlink sniffer.

<Note>
A 5G/4G sniffer observes the *setup* (rogue cell + rejects + cascade), which is sufficient to alarm. Observing the *final interception* on the 2G leg requires a separate GSM-capable receiver — but detection does not need it.
</Note>

## Detection & Mitigation

**UE / OS side**
- **Disable 2G** — Android 14+ exposes an "Allow 2G" toggle; turning it off breaks the final leg outright. iOS does not yet expose this to users.
- A UE that receives a pre-auth `Registration Reject` cause 27 from a same-PLMN cell which then disappears is behaving suspiciously and could self-flag.

**Operator side**
- **Set the GERAN-redirect policy bit** — TS 24.301 §9.9.3.52 "Unsecured redirection to GERAN not allowed" blocks the 4G→2G RRC redirect. *No operator the authors tested had enabled it.*
- Watch for registration→reject storms and sudden RAT-distribution shifts (mass drop to 2G) within a cell.

**Specification (authors' proposal)**
- Accept **high-impact NAS reject causes only after authentication** — the root fix.
- Reject the connection on a `HashMME`/`HashUE` mismatch; tighten replay and Initial-NAS-message protection.
