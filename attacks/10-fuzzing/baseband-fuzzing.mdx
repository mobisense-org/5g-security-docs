## Why This Cluster Matters

For years, baseband fuzzing was bottlenecked by two problems:

1. **Emulation lacked realism** — Shannon/MediaTek emulators couldn't run the full protocol state machine, so fuzzers found shallow bugs only.
2. **Over-the-air testing was slow** — running mutations through real radios is orders of magnitude slower than emulation and hard to reproduce.

In 2025 four papers tackled these gaps, collectively reopening commercial baseband as a productive bug-hunting target.

## The Four Papers

| Paper | Venue | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Stateful Analysis and Fuzzing of Commercial Baseband Firmware** | IEEE S&P 2025 | First scalable stateful fuzzer for commercial basebands; protocol-state coverage instead of code coverage |
| **BaseBridge** | IEEE S&P 2025 | Bridges emulation and over-the-air testing — bugs found in emulation are auto-validated against real radios |
| **LLFuzz** | 2025 | Over-the-air dynamic testing focused on lower-layer (PHY/MAC/RLC) protocol handling |
| **FirmState** | WiSec 2025 | Adds protocol-state semantics to Shannon (Samsung Exynos) baseband emulation |

## What's Actually New

- **Stateful analysis** at the firmware level: fuzzers now reason about RRC connection states, NAS security context, and registration state simultaneously — not just message syntax.
- **Hybrid emulation + OTA pipelines**: BaseBridge replays interesting emulator inputs through a real B210 + UE so a software-found "bug" can be confirmed/denied on hardware in seconds.
- **Lower-layer focus**: LLFuzz attacks PHY/MAC/RLC, where most existing fuzzers don't reach because they require precise timing.
- **Vendor coverage**: FirmState specifically targets Samsung Shannon basebands, complementing the Qualcomm/MediaTek-heavy 5GHOUL line of work.

## Threat Model

Same as 5GHOUL: attacker operates a rogue gNB (USRP B210 + srsRAN/Open5GS) or runs the firmware in emulation. No SIM, no auth — bugs land before the security context is established.

## Reproduction Notes

- **FirmState** and the **Stateful Analysis** paper publish enough detail to rebuild against open Shannon dumps; expect significant reverse-engineering effort.
- **BaseBridge** workflow is the most practical entry point: emulate cheaply, validate selectively over the air.
- **LLFuzz** requires SDR + a target UE; setup is closer to 5GHOUL's complexity.

## Why You Should Care

If you tracked 5GHOUL (Dec 2023) and 5GBaseChecker (Aug 2024) in our [main fuzzing list](/attacks/10-fuzzing/overview), this 2025 wave is the natural follow-up. Expect a fresh round of CVE-bearing baseband bugs disclosed through 2026 — these tools are the engine.
